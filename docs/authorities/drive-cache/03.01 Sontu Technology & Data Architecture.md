SONTU TECHNOLOGY & DATA ARCHITECTURE

Status: GATE 1 TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE AUTHORITY  
Date: 2026-09-02

Authority role  
This document owns Joy’s technology and data architecture: system boundaries, domain persistence, identity/authentication, authorization, organization permissions, payments/admission integrity, communications delivery, security, auditability, telemetry, reliability and environment strategy. It implements—not redefines—the approved Product, Event Model, Scope, Journey and Experience authorities.

1 — ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLE  
Joy should begin as a coherent modular application backed by one authoritative transactional data system rather than a prematurely distributed microservice estate.

Separate services are justified when a boundary has materially different security, reliability, scaling, provider or operational requirements—not because a legacy diagram used microservices.

The architecture must preserve domain boundaries in code/data even when deployed together.

2 — SYSTEM SURFACES  
Client surfaces:  
\- Joy mobile application.  
\- Joy public web/authenticated web surface where approved.  
\- Internal admin/support surface.

Backend responsibilities:  
\- Authentication/session integration.  
\- Domain API/business logic.  
\- Transactional persistence.  
\- Authorization and policy enforcement.  
\- Payment provider integration.  
\- Admission/credential verification.  
\- Notification/communication orchestration.  
\- File/media handling where approved.  
\- Audit/event history.  
\- Product/operational telemetry.  
\- Administrative/support operations.

3 — DOMAIN BOUNDARIES  
Primary domain modules should include:  
\- Accounts & Identity.  
\- Organizations & Membership.  
\- Events.  
\- Access & Eligibility.  
\- Participation / Registration.  
\- Payments & Orders.  
\- Admissions / Credentials.  
\- Attendance / Check-in.  
\- Communications.  
\- Audit & Operational History.  
\- Trust / Safety / Support.  
\- Telemetry.

These are conceptual ownership boundaries. They do not require separate databases or services.

4 — AUTHORITATIVE DATA MODEL PRINCIPLES  
Use stable unique identifiers for durable domain entities.

Account identity, organization identity, event ownership, participation, payment, admission credential and attendance are distinct records/concepts. Do not collapse them into a single user-event status field.

Current state may be materialized for efficient reads, but consequential transitions should preserve enough history to explain what happened.

Server-authoritative state governs permissions, capacity, payment confirmation, credential validity and check-in outcomes.

Client caches are convenience copies, never authority for consequential actions.

5 — CORE ENTITY FAMILIES  
Account  
Authenticated human identity and account-level preferences/status.

Organization  
Durable host entity independent of any individual member.

Organization Membership  
Relationship between account and organization, including approved role/permission semantics.

Event  
Durable event identity, owner reference, category/context, format, lifecycle and configuration references.

Event Access Configuration  
Visibility, access permission and eligibility/restriction configuration without conflating these concepts.

Participation / Registration  
Attendee relationship to event: RSVP/registration/invite participation and lifecycle independent of payment/check-in.

Order / Payment  
Financial intent and provider reconciliation records for paid admission.

Admission Credential  
Entitlement/credential created only when admission conditions are satisfied. QR or equivalent verification representation references this entitlement rather than embedding trusted truth solely in the client.

Attendance / Check-in  
Operational admission/check-in events separate from registration and payment.

Communication  
Host-to-participant operational messages and delivery state.

Audit Event  
Consequential actor/action/target/time/context record.

6 — EVENT STATE  
Event lifecycle semantics are inherited from 02.01.01 Joy Event Model & Capabilities.

Technical implementation must prevent invalid transitions and must not invent UI-only lifecycle meanings. Material state transitions should be transactional and auditable.

Archiving/history is record-management behavior, not a replacement for the event lifecycle.

7 — AUTHENTICATION  
Unrestricted public event viewing must not depend on authentication.

Authentication is required where identity is legitimately necessary, including protected/private access, participant-specific records, host operations, organization administration and other approved consequential actions.

Requirements:  
\- Secure session lifecycle.  
\- Session revocation capability.  
\- Account recovery.  
\- Email/phone/social authentication choices remain implementation decisions until provider selection.  
\- Authentication return-to-intent support so a user can resume the intended event/action.  
\- Authentication failure/cancellation cannot mutate event, registration or payment state.

8 — AUTHORIZATION  
Authorization must be enforced server-side for every protected operation.

Do not rely on hidden buttons, client routes or obscured URLs as access control.

Authorization should evaluate actor, target resource, ownership/membership, role/permission, event access configuration and relevant state.

Default deny where no permission is established.

9 — ORGANIZATION PERMISSIONS  
Beta architecture requires a practical role model rather than enterprise RBAC complexity.

Recommended baseline roles:  
\- Owner — organization control, membership administration and consequential ownership-level actions.  
\- Admin/Manager — broad event operations and appropriate member/event management without organization ownership transfer unless explicitly permitted.  
\- Event Operator — scoped operational access to assigned events, such as participant management/check-in/communications where granted.

Exact UI labels may change through testing. Technical permissions should be capability-based beneath the role presets so future roles do not require schema redesign.

Removing a member or changing permission must take effect server-side without waiting for stale client state. Organization-owned events remain organization-owned after member removal.

10 — PUBLIC, PRIVATE & RESTRICTED DATA  
Public web endpoints should return only explicitly public event data.

Unlisted link knowledge is not equivalent to private/restricted authorization.

Private and restricted data must be fetched through authorized paths. Protected virtual links, participant data, eligibility evidence and operational details must never be embedded in public page payloads merely hidden by the UI.

Eligibility verification architecture must support the assurance method later approved by Legal/Safety without requiring Joy to expose unnecessary sensitive evidence across the product.

11 — PARTICIPATION & CAPACITY  
Registration/RSVP writes must be server-authoritative and concurrency-safe.

When capacity applies, availability must be revalidated during commitment. Two clients observing the last available spot cannot both succeed merely because each cached availability.

Participation transitions should be idempotent where retries are plausible.

Waitlists are not assumed unless promoted into scope.

12 — PAYMENT ARCHITECTURE  
Use an external compliant payment provider rather than storing raw payment-card data in Joy.

Provider selection remains downstream implementation/business/legal work, but architecture must support:  
\- Payment intent/order creation.  
\- Provider-side confirmation.  
\- Webhook/event reconciliation.  
\- Idempotency.  
\- Duplicate-event handling.  
\- Failure and pending/unknown outcomes.  
\- Refund/cancellation reconciliation.  
\- Transaction/audit history.  
\- Fee/tax/payout data as required by the eventual merchant model.

A client success callback is not sufficient evidence of payment.

Admission credentials must not become valid until server-side payment/admission conditions are confirmed.

13 — ADMISSION CREDENTIALS & QR  
QR is a presentation/verification mechanism, not the source of truth.

Credential verification should resolve to server-authoritative admission state and event context.

Requirements:  
\- Credential cannot be forged by changing client-visible fields.  
\- Invalid/cancelled/refunded credential is denied.  
\- Duplicate scan is detected deterministically.  
\- Re-entry policy, when enabled, is explicit rather than accidental.  
\- Manual lookup/fallback remains permission-controlled and auditable.  
\- Degraded-connectivity admission/check-in requires an explicit fallback strategy before external physical-event testing. The exact mechanism may be staged, but any local cache, manual/printed fallback or offline-capable decision path must define staleness bounds, duplicate-use handling, conflict resolution and reconciliation back to server-authoritative truth. Joy does not assume full offline authority by default.

14 — COMMUNICATIONS  
Joy should model an operational communication once and track delivery across provider channels rather than coupling domain semantics to one notification vendor.

Initial communication types include approved announcements, reminders, material changes and cancellations.

Requirements:  
\- Audience resolution is server-side.  
\- Delivery attempts/status are observable.  
\- User communication preferences apply where legally/product-appropriate.  
\- Critical transactional/operational messaging rules remain distinguishable from optional marketing.  
\- Provider retries do not create duplicate user-visible messages where preventable.

Realtime chat infrastructure is not part of the baseline architecture unless scope changes.

15 — FILES & MEDIA  
Media storage should be object/file storage with metadata references in transactional data rather than large binary payloads in core tables.

Uploads require type/size validation and access rules appropriate to the media’s visibility.

Public event media and protected evidence/media must not share accidental public access semantics.

16 — AUDITABILITY  
Consequential operations require durable auditability proportionate to risk.

Examples:  
\- Publish/cancel/material event change.  
\- Ownership/organization permission changes.  
\- Restricted-event administrative actions.  
\- Payment/refund reconciliation.  
\- Credential invalidation/check-in override.  
\- Admin/support moderation actions.

Audit records should capture actor, action, target, timestamp and useful context/correlation without indiscriminately copying sensitive payloads.

17 — TELEMETRY  
Product analytics and operational observability are separate but complementary.

Product telemetry should measure approved funnel/behavior events such as create start/completion, publish, public event view, registration/RSVP, checkout progression, confirmed purchase, credential retrieval, check-in and event completion where observable.

Operational telemetry should include errors, latency, failed jobs/webhooks, authorization denials, payment reconciliation failures, communication failures and check-in verification failures.

Telemetry event names/properties should be versioned/stable enough for regression analysis. Do not log secrets, raw payment data or unnecessary sensitive eligibility information.

18 — SECURITY BASELINE  
Required principles:  
\- TLS in transit.  
\- Secure secret management; no production secrets in repository/client builds.  
\- Least privilege for infrastructure/service access.  
\- Server-side input validation.  
\- Parameterized/ORM-safe database access.  
\- Strong authorization boundaries.  
\- Rate/abuse controls on sensitive public/auth endpoints as justified.  
\- Dependency/security scanning in CI.  
\- Audit logging for consequential actions.  
\- Secure webhook verification.  
\- Environment separation.  
\- Backup/recovery appropriate to production criticality.

Exact controls should be refined through the Security/Production-readiness workstream rather than copied from legacy architecture.

19 — PRIVACY BY ARCHITECTURE  
Collect data because a defined product/operational/legal need exists, not because it may be useful later.

Participant registration fields should be constrained rather than enabling arbitrary sensitive-data collection by default.

Data classification and retention rules must be established before external testing for categories that collect protected or sensitive information.

Deletion/account closure requirements must distinguish account data from records Joy may need to retain for transaction, fraud, audit or legal obligations.

20 — RELIABILITY & IDEMPOTENCY  
Consequential write APIs/jobs should be safe against common retry/duplicate patterns.

Priority idempotent operations include:  
\- Registration/participation commitment.  
\- Payment/order creation.  
\- Payment provider webhook processing.  
\- Refund processing requests where applicable.  
\- Credential issuance.  
\- Communication job dispatch.  
\- Check-in writes where network retry could duplicate an operation.

When the system cannot determine whether an external operation completed, persist/communicate an explicit pending or reconciliation-required state rather than guessing.

21 — BACKGROUND WORK  
Use background jobs for operations that do not need to block an interactive request, such as notification delivery, webhook follow-up, media processing and noncritical analytics propagation.

Jobs need retry policy, deduplication/idempotency where relevant, observable failure/dead-letter handling and correlation to originating domain actions.

22 — ENVIRONMENTS  
Minimum lifecycle:  
\- Local/development.  
\- Test/CI.  
\- Staging or equivalent pre-production environment.  
\- Production.

External provider test/sandbox credentials must be separated from production credentials.

Production data should not be casually copied into lower environments. Test fixtures/synthetic data should support E2E testing.

23 — REPOSITORY & DELIVERY  
Use Git/GitHub with protected primary integration/release practices appropriate to team size.

Required direction:  
\- Automated lint/type/static checks where stack supports them.  
\- Automated unit/integration tests for domain rules.  
\- Migration/version control for schema changes.  
\- CI build/test before production deployment.  
\- Reproducible environment configuration.  
\- Rollback/forward-fix strategy.  
\- No manual production database edits as a normal product workflow.

Exact branch naming from legacy material is not inherited.

24 — API / SERVER CONTRACT PRINCIPLES  
APIs should expose domain operations and stable resource contracts rather than mirroring database tables blindly.

Requirements:  
\- Explicit validation errors.  
\- Stable error categories for clients.  
\- Pagination for growing collections.  
\- Server timestamps for consequential events.  
\- Correlation/request identifiers where operationally useful.  
\- Versioning/evolution strategy that avoids breaking released clients casually.

25 — CACHING & CLIENT STATE  
Cache only where it improves experience or efficiency without weakening correctness.

Public event pages may use cache/CDN strategies with deliberate invalidation for material changes/cancellation.

Protected participant, permission, payment and credential truth should be refreshed/revalidated at consequential boundaries.

26 — ADMIN & SUPPORT ARCHITECTURE  
Internal support/admin operations require explicit privileged authorization, auditability and narrow tools for actual operational needs.

Do not create a parallel admin authentication universe unless there is a security reason. Prefer the same identity foundation with stronger privileged roles/policies and appropriate additional safeguards.

27 — INITIAL IMPLEMENTATION DIRECTION  
Gate 1 should select a stack that optimizes coherent development, transactional correctness, security and low operational overhead for the expected early scale.

Do not inherit Flutter/Firebase/Node/Firestore/Postgres/AWS combinations from legacy planning.

A relational transactional database is strongly preferred for the core Joy domain because ownership, permissions, registration/capacity, payments, credentials and audit relationships benefit from relational integrity and transactions.

The approved implementation direction is defined in Section 31 and inherited by the Repository Manifest, Prototype Implementation Manifest and 03.04 Joy Supabase Schema & Migration Plan. Provider choices deliberately staged in Section 36 remain unresolved only for the capabilities that depend on them.

31 — GATE 1 STACK & PROVIDER DECISIONS

Decision status: approved implementation direction for prototype and Working Beta unless implementation evidence produces a material reason to change it.

Core backend/data platform — Supabase  
Use Supabase as Joy’s initial backend platform: PostgreSQL for authoritative relational data, Supabase Auth for account/session foundation, Storage for approved files/media, Row Level Security as a defense-in-depth authorization layer, and Edge Functions/server-side functions where privileged or provider-facing operations require trusted execution.

Rationale: Joy’s domain is relational and transaction-heavy; the existing architecture already prefers PostgreSQL. Supabase consolidates database, auth, storage and server capabilities without forcing a microservice estate. It also keeps the data model portable at the PostgreSQL layer rather than binding core semantics to a proprietary document database.

Production rule: RLS does not replace domain authorization design. Privileged server operations, payment reconciliation, capacity-sensitive commits and other consequential writes still require explicit trusted server/database logic and tests.

Client/application direction — TypeScript \+ React family  
Use TypeScript as the primary application language. Build the public/authenticated web surface with a React-based server-capable web framework suitable for public event pages, deep links and authenticated application routes. Build the mobile application with React Native/Expo unless Gate 2 prototype evidence exposes a material platform limitation.

The web and mobile clients should share domain types, validation contracts and non-visual application logic where practical, but should not force one UI implementation across fundamentally different surfaces.

Repository direction — monorepo  
Prefer one Joy repository with workspace packages/apps for web, mobile, shared domain/contracts and server/provider integration code. Keep module boundaries explicit. Do not create separate repositories/services without a concrete security, deployment or ownership reason.

Hosting — managed web hosting \+ Supabase backend  
Use a managed React-compatible hosting/CDN platform for the public web/application frontend and Supabase for the backend/data plane. Exact frontend host can be selected during repository bootstrap based on framework choice and deployment fit; changing the frontend host is not a product-architecture change if contracts remain intact.

Authentication — Supabase Auth  
Use Supabase Auth initially. Begin with email-based authentication appropriate to testing; add OAuth/social providers only when they reduce real user friction. Phone/SMS authentication is not required for initial Beta and should not be introduced merely because event products often use phone numbers.

Account profile/domain records remain separate from provider auth identity so authentication implementation can evolve without redefining Joy’s domain model.

Transactional email — Resend preferred  
Use Resend as the initial transactional email provider for account/event operational email, subject to normal deliverability/domain setup and a provider review before external production use. Keep Joy communication records/provider delivery state vendor-neutral so Resend can be replaced without changing communication semantics.

Push notifications — platform push through Expo during Beta  
For the Expo/React Native direction, use Expo-compatible push delivery initially. Treat push as a delivery channel beneath Joy’s Communication domain. Do not make a push token or provider response the authoritative communication record.

SMS — deferred  
SMS is not baseline Beta infrastructure. Introduce an SMS provider only when a validated operational or authentication use case justifies the cost/compliance surface.

Payments — Stripe Connect preferred for paid-event architecture  
Stripe Connect is the preferred provider direction for marketplace/platform-style host payments because Joy needs to onboard hosts, accept attendee payments, reconcile provider state and eventually route payouts while keeping platform/host responsibilities explicit.

This is a provider direction, not a final merchant-of-record/legal conclusion. Before paid external testing, Business/Legal must choose the actual Connect charge/payout model and responsibility allocation. Until then, paid-event implementation remains capability-gated.

Payment implementation must use provider-authoritative confirmation/webhooks, idempotency and reconciliation as already required by this architecture.

Product analytics — lightweight event instrumentation first  
Implement a small stable Joy product-event schema in the application from Gate 2/3 onward. Avoid binding domain behavior to an analytics vendor. A hosted analytics product may be selected when the Beta needs cohort/funnel exploration; raw critical operational truth remains in Joy’s database/audit records.

Error tracking / observability — Sentry-compatible direction  
Use structured application error tracking suitable for React/React Native and server functions; Sentry is the preferred initial direction. Keep structured server logs and provider/job failure records in their appropriate systems as well. Error tracking is not a replacement for audit history or product telemetry.

Source control / CI — GitHub  
Use GitHub as source control and GitHub Actions or equivalent repository-native CI for lint/type/test/build/migration checks. Drive remains product/requirements/governance authority under Joy governance; consequential implementation discoveries reconcile back to Drive.

Secrets  
Use provider/environment secret stores. No production secret belongs in client code, committed repository files or Drive planning documents.

32 — ENVIRONMENT TOPOLOGY DECISION

Gate 2 Prototype may use local/development services and a dedicated non-production Supabase project where persistence is useful.

Gate 3 must have isolated development/test and staging/Beta data boundaries appropriate to the selected Supabase plan/project structure. Production/external-test credentials and data must not be casually reused in local development.

Before Gate 4, establish an explicit production project/environment with production secrets, backups/recovery configuration, provider production accounts and controlled feature gates.

Do not create a large multi-environment estate before it is needed; separation must increase with consequence.

33 — DATA ACCESS / SERVER EXECUTION DECISION

Use PostgreSQL constraints, transactions and database/server functions for invariants that must remain correct under concurrency. Use RLS for row-level access enforcement where it maps cleanly to authenticated context.

Do not place payment-provider secrets, privileged organization administration, moderation overrides or other trusted operations in public clients.

Capacity commitment, credential issuance, permission-sensitive mutations and provider reconciliation should have explicit server-authoritative transaction boundaries.

34 — BACKGROUND WORK DECISION

Begin with the simplest reliable managed/server-side mechanism supported by the selected stack for asynchronous notification/provider reconciliation work. Do not add Kafka, RabbitMQ or a separate queue cluster during early Beta without demonstrated need.

Jobs must still meet the retry/idempotency/observability requirements in Section 21\. If workload/reliability evidence exceeds the initial mechanism, introduce a dedicated queue as an architectural evolution.

35 — PROVIDER PORTABILITY RULE

Joy does not need provider abstraction layers everywhere. Abstract where the provider is an external delivery/financial/identity boundary likely to evolve or where domain truth must remain vendor-neutral.

Keep these boundaries provider-neutral in the domain model:  
\- Authentication identity linkage.  
\- Communication/delivery records.  
\- Payment/order/provider references.  
\- File/media metadata.  
\- Analytics events.  
\- Eligibility assurance results/evidence references.

Do not create speculative generic interfaces around ordinary PostgreSQL persistence merely to make theoretical provider replacement easy.

37 — ENGINEERING QUALITY, PERFORMANCE & RECOVERY REQUIREMENTS

Performance targets must be derived from supported devices, real journeys and measured infrastructure rather than arbitrary inherited numbers. Even before exact service-level targets are approved, Joy should explicitly measure client startup/render responsiveness, public event-page performance, consequential API latency, error/crash rate, availability and concurrency behavior under realistic event spikes. Load/stress testing should cover event access, registration/capacity and check-in/payment paths as those capabilities activate.

Broad product capability must not become broad runtime payload. Capability and route boundaries should allow code/data to load according to the active event context. Use progressive module loading where useful, capability-driven data access, bounded/paginated collection queries and explicit performance budgets for critical surfaces. A simple event must not hydrate specialized exhibitor, sponsor, staff, advanced ticketing or unrelated historical data merely because those capabilities exist elsewhere in Joy.

Localization implementation must use externalized locale resources rather than hard-coded user-facing strings. Locale switching should not require reinstalling the application, and date/time/timezone formatting must use locale-aware platform/library behavior. Exact launch languages remain governed by Product/Market requirements rather than technical precedent.

Backup/recovery architecture must support secure automated backups appropriate to production criticality, restoration testing and an off-environment/offsite protection strategy where justified. Exact backup frequency, retention, RPO and RTO are production-readiness decisions and must be evidence/risk based.

Delivery/repository planning must eventually define a practical branch/integration strategy, automated build/test/deployment, staging/pre-production deployment, production deployment controls, monitoring/error reporting, semantic or otherwise explicit release versioning, and maintained repository README/API/developer documentation. Exact branch names and release cadence belong to the later Repository Manifest and release planning rather than this architecture.

Scalability should favor managed horizontal scaling/CDN/caching capabilities available in the selected stack and measurement-driven optimization. Do not pre-plan database sharding or microservice extraction at arbitrary record thresholds; preserve the ability to evolve when measured bottlenecks justify it.

Authority correction  
This document owns candidate technical and data principles for Sontu. It is downstream of Sontu Governance, 01.03 Sontu Product Foundation, 02.01.01 Sontu Event Model & Capability Catalog, 02.01.03 Sontu Validation & Product Roadmap, and applicable experience/journey authorities. It may constrain implementation for correctness, security, privacy, reliability and operability, but it does not independently select product scope, activate capabilities, open a gate or authorize implementation.

Legacy interpretation  
Sections above this refoundation authority contain substantial reusable architecture work. Their domain-boundary, transactional-correctness, authorization, privacy, auditability, idempotency, provider-reconciliation, environment-separation and reliability principles remain strong candidate requirements where the corresponding Sontu capability exists. However, Joy-era statements declaring Gate 1 architecture complete, Gate 2 unblocked, specific providers approved, Beta scope fixed, implementation manifests authoritative or prototype work ready are historical and non-binding. No provider, framework, repository topology or deployment choice is active merely because Joy selected it.

One governed event-state architecture  
Sontu must maintain one authoritative event-domain state across Participant Web/App, Host Web/App, Advanced Host Control Center, Role-Specific Operational Mode, public/accountless web, internal admin/support and future AI/Agent/API interfaces. A surface may optimize presentation, caching and workflows for its actor and device, but may not create a parallel source of truth for event lifecycle, ownership, permissions, participation/commitment, entitlement, capacity, payment state, changes, attendance or operational history.

Architecture shape  
Prefer a coherent modular system with explicit domain boundaries and an authoritative transactional core over premature microservices or duplicated per-surface backends. Deployment boundaries should be earned by materially different security, scaling, reliability, provider or operational needs. Sontu’s broad canonical capability library must not imply broad initial runtime scope.

Canonical conceptual domain families  
Technical modeling should be able to represent, when activated:  
\- identity and accounts without making accounts universal;  
\- people/parties and participant relationships;  
\- organizations, membership, ownership and delegated authority;  
\- events, composite/sub-events, lifecycle and recurrence/series relationships;  
\- context/category/format/visibility/discoverability without allowing category to dictate capability;  
\- access, eligibility, assurance and privacy boundaries;  
\- commitment states beyond RSVP, including invitation, interest, application, approval, registration, reservation, waitlist, cancellation/release and reconfirmation where relevant;  
\- capacity/inventory and allocation;  
\- orders, payments, refunds, chargebacks and payouts where relevant;  
\- entitlements and credentials as distinct concepts;  
\- attendance/check-in and operational exceptions;  
\- program/schedule/activities;  
\- event changes, impact/audience resolution and communications;  
\- tasks/operations and scoped operational roles;  
\- event-scoped interaction/media;  
\- accessibility/accommodation data with proportional exposure and retention;  
\- audit/history, outcomes and telemetry.  
These are conceptual boundaries, not a requirement for separate services, schemas or initial-build modules.

Accountless-first technical rule  
Public understanding and low-consequence permitted actions should not require an account merely for architectural convenience. Where Sontu supports accountless participation or media contribution, the architecture must still provide secure scoped tokens/links or equivalent mechanisms, expiry/revocation where appropriate, abuse controls and a deliberate path for later identity association when the user chooses or consequence requires it. Authentication strength scales with consequence.

Ownership and authorization  
Event ownership must be durable and explicit. Organization-owned events remain owned by the organization independent of individual operator membership. Authorization is server-authoritative and capability/resource/state aware; UI visibility is never access control. Role presets may simplify UX, but underlying permissions should remain scoped enough to support standard hosts, advanced operators and specialized event roles without granting broad unnecessary access.

Commitment, capacity and entitlement correctness  
Commitment state, financial state, entitlement and attendance remain distinct. Capacity-sensitive actions must be concurrency-safe and server-authoritative. Entitlement is the authoritative right to admission/access; QR, ticket, badge, wallet pass or similar artifacts are credentials/presentations of that right. Retries and ambiguous external outcomes must resolve through idempotency and reconciliation rather than optimistic client assumptions.

Change propagation architecture  
Material event change is a domain operation, not merely a message. The system must be able to determine affected state/audiences, commit the authoritative change, preserve audit history and orchestrate appropriate downstream communication/reconfirmation/refund/access effects. Cached/public representations need deliberate invalidation or freshness behavior for consequential changes.

Payments boundary  
Sontu may own the event-commerce workflow and state while a specialist regulated provider owns payment rails. Provider-authoritative confirmation, signed/verified callbacks or webhooks, idempotency, reconciliation and explicit pending/unknown states are required before payment-dependent entitlements become authoritative. Exact provider, merchant model, taxes, payouts and commercial allocation are later evidence/legal/business decisions unless a selected validation scenario requires them.

Media/storage boundary  
Event-scoped media should use appropriate object/file storage with transactional metadata and explicit visibility/retention rules. Accountless guest contribution may be supported where risk permits. Temporary, sensitive and evidence media must not inherit accidental public access or indefinite retention. Additional storage can be a commercial add-on without altering event-domain truth.

Advanced Host Control Center  
The Control Center must extend the same APIs/domain model as standard host surfaces. Floorplans/seating, complex schedules, booths/exhibitors, staffing and other advanced capabilities should be modeled as optional capability modules when earned; they must not require a parallel professional event database. No Control Center module is an initial implementation obligation unless Gate 1 validation design requires it.

Role-Specific Operational Mode  
Operational clients should receive only the data/actions needed for their assigned role and event context. Door/check-in, staff, volunteer, speaker and exhibitor/vendor interfaces may require different caching, latency or degraded-connectivity strategies, but reconciliation must return to authoritative event state. Offline authority is never assumed globally.

participant\_reconfirm(token, applicable\_version, decision, idempotency\_key): resolves only that participant’s applicable reconfirmation; stale-version response cannot satisfy newer requirement.

accept\_suggested\_consequence(case\_id, expected\_row\_version, idempotency\_key): owner only; transitions suggested candidate into managed unresolved state if still applicable.  
dismiss\_suggested\_consequence(...): owner only; records explicit disposition/reason without deleting history.  
waive\_consequence(...): owner only when waivable policy permits; WAIVED remains distinct from RESOLVED.  
reconcile\_provider\_evidence(source identity, external evidence id, applicable version, status, idempotency key): trusted adapter only; deduplicates replay; records evidence; recomputes only requirements that evidence is allowed to satisfy; UNKNOWN/PENDING/FAILED/STALE never promoted to success.  
record\_communication\_result(...): trusted adapter; updates communication attempt/result only; cannot directly settle a consequence unless a separate deterministic requirement explicitly treats a verified communication outcome as sufficient evidence.  
reopen\_or\_replace\_consequence(...): governed function; preserves terminal history and lineage; new material version or invalidated evidence can reopen/replace rather than overwrite old terminal state.  
cancel\_event(...): owner only; consequential, version-checked, audited; generates only cancellation obligations required by frozen policy.

6\. SETTLEMENT EVALUATOR CONTRACT  
Settlement is a deterministic function over the case’s active requirements and admissible evidence. AI text, notification intent, draft text, provider attempt or host dashboard display cannot satisfy a requirement by themselves. Each requirement defines admissible evidence kinds, applicable event version and terminal predicate. Case RESOLVED requires every mandatory active requirement to reach a permitted terminal outcome. WAIVED and EXCEPTION are separate terminal dispositions. A newer material event version or invalidation of relied-upon evidence triggers deterministic re-evaluation and may create REOPEN/REPLACE lineage.

PROFILE & SOCIAL RELATIONSHIP DATA ARCHITECTURE RECONCILIATION — founder authority

Purpose  
This section consumes the current founder-locked Profiles, Connections, Following & Host Reputation UX/domain authority. It establishes implementation/data constraints without prescribing a final physical schema or prematurely locking reputation algorithms.

Identity keys  
All durable social and reputation relationships must reference immutable account/entity identifiers. @handles, display names, email addresses and other mutable/addressable attributes must not be primary relationship keys. Handle changes must not break Follow, Connection, Group, event, host-role or reputation relationships.

Relationship separation  
Authoritative persistence must distinguish at least these concepts rather than overload one friendship table/state: unilateral Follow; Connection request/direction and reciprocal accepted Connection; owner-private Close Connection classification; owner-private custom Group; Group membership; blocking/safety relationships according to their governing authority; and event-derived/shared-context evidence where persisted rather than computed.

Follow persistence  
A Follow record represents follower immutable ID → followed immutable person/entity ID plus authoritative lifecycle/audit fields appropriate to implementation. Uniqueness/idempotency must prevent duplicate active follows for the same pair. Unfollow must be safely repeatable and must not delete unrelated event/audit history. Following oneself should be prohibited unless a later explicit non-person entity model creates a legitimate distinct acting identity.

Connection persistence  
Connection requests require enough state to distinguish requester and recipient and to resolve pending, accepted, declined and removed outcomes safely. An accepted Connection is semantically reciprocal even if physical storage uses one canonical pair row or another normalized representation. Duplicate/crossing requests must reconcile deterministically rather than create multiple Connections. Removing a Connection must not delete historical events/invitations shared by the pair.

Close classification and Groups  
Close Connection is owner-private metadata referencing an active Connection/person relationship and must not be readable as a reciprocal/public attribute. Custom Groups belong to one owner identity. Group membership supports multiple Groups per Connection and multiple Connections per Group. Deleting/renaming a Group changes only the owner's organization metadata and must not mutate events or audiences already materialized for prior invitations.

Invitation audience snapshot rule  
When a host selects a reusable social audience such as a Group, Close Connections or another permitted relationship-derived audience, the eventual event invitation/recipient records must preserve the actual resolved recipients/relationship truth needed for that event. Later edits to the Group or Connection graph must not silently rewrite historical invitations or participant relationships.

Privacy-aware query boundary  
Search, Profile, Discover, Feed, invitation tools and shared-event discovery must evaluate current authorization/privacy/safety rules before exposing relationship-derived facts. Data availability in storage is not permission to display it. Queries must not leak protected event membership through follower graphs, mutual counts, shared-event labels, recommendation explanations, notification payloads or aggregate endpoints.

Counts and aggregates  
Follower/Following counts may be cached/materialized for performance, but authoritative relationship records remain the source of truth and aggregate repair/reconciliation must be possible. Counts should not include relationships suppressed/invalid under the applicable account lifecycle rules. Do not make follower counts authorization inputs.

Shared Event Context  
Prefer deriving shared-event context from authoritative event relationships when feasible. If materialized for performance, store only the minimum provenance needed to reproduce/validate the context and apply expiry/recomputation where source visibility or relationship state can change. A materialized mutual/shared-event edge must never become an independent authorization grant.

Host reputation evidence architecture  
Reputation should be evidence-derived rather than a mutable vanity score written directly by clients. Preserve source facts/provenance sufficient to recompute future host-quality signals from authoritative event/attendance/follow/feedback/reliability evidence. Candidate derived metrics may include completed events, credible attendees, repeat-attendee measures, post-attendance Follow measures, verified-feedback aggregates and reliability outcomes. Keep raw/source evidence semantically separate from derived metrics/designations so formulas can evolve without rewriting history.

Attendance provenance  
Host-reputation calculations must consume the authoritative attendance/evidence model rather than infer attendance merely from RSVP, registration, ticket issuance, QR possession or absence of a scan. Where attendance confidence/evidence type exists, derived metrics should preserve that distinction or eligibility threshold.

Post-attendance Follow provenance  
If used, attendee-to-follow analysis should be derivable from event host identity, credible attendance/participation evidence and Follow creation time/provenance. Do not expose a person's private attendance history merely because the derived aggregate contributes to host reputation. Aggregate computation must enforce minimum cohort/privacy rules before public presentation.

Feedback architecture  
Feedback used for public host reputation should be bound to a legitimate event/participant relationship and designed for one authoritative contribution per eligible participant/event/feedback dimension as policy defines. Store sufficient provenance for moderation/abuse handling and recomputation. Public aggregates require configurable minimum sample/confidence thresholds. Exact rating scale, questions, weighting and threshold are not locked here.

Super Host derivation  
Super Host must be represented as a derived/evidence-backed designation with versionable qualification logic and explainable provenance sufficient for support/audit. Do not encode it as a permanent account type. Qualification logic must be changeable as evidence develops, and historical qualification periods should remain distinguishable from current status where later policy requires it. Exact algorithm is intentionally deferred.

Anti-abuse and rate-control hooks  
Architecture must permit policy controls for Follow/Connection request velocity, spam invitations, duplicate/self-manufactured reputation activity, block evasion and other abuse without redesigning the core relationship model. Exact limits remain policy-configurable rather than scattered UI constants.

Deletion, privacy and retention  
Account deletion/privacy workflows must distinguish current social-graph removal from event/audit/history retention obligations. Relationship records and derived reputation evidence must follow the applicable privacy/retention authority; historical event truth must not be corrupted merely to remove a current Follow/Connection. Public caches/aggregates must be invalidatable when governing state changes.

Extensibility boundary  
Initial implementation may ship only the subset required by current product scope, but schema/API design should not force Follow=Connection, public Group membership, host-only Follow targets, or a fixed Super Host formula. Do not implement general DMs, personal-post timelines or influencer analytics merely because the relationship graph exists.
